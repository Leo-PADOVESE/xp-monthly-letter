In February 2025, Brazil's macroeconomic outlook reveals a cautiously optimistic environment amid signs of economic deceleration. Following a robust growth of 3.6% in 2024, the GDP is projected to advance by only 2.0% in 2025, with further slowing to 1.0% in 2026, primarily due to the lagged effects of a contractionary monetary policy. The inflation rate is expected to remain elevated, with projections for the IPCA at 6.1% in 2025 before easing to 4.5% in 2026. The Central Bank's policy stance remains stringent, with the Selic rate anticipated to peak at 15.50%, reflecting ongoing inflationary pressures. While the external environment has improved slightly, bolstered by recovering commodity prices and delayed U.S. tariffs, domestic factors such as rising public debt and a weakening fiscal outlook pose significant risks. As a result, market sentiment remains cautious, with investors advised to monitor potential volatility stemming from political dynamics ahead of the upcoming elections.
